Review:
This paper presents LETT-NeXt, a lightweight RECIST-guided model for 3D CT lesion segmentation. The framework combines RECIST-centered cropping, prompt-channel conditioning, a compact MedNeXt-v2 encoder-decoder, auxiliary anatomy-tumor supervision, and adaptive AutoZoom inference. On the public validation set, LETT-NeXt achieves a Dice Similarity Coefficient of 79.43% and a Normalized Surface Dice of 72.32%. On the hidden test set, it achieves a challenge score of 70.60%.

Strengths: The paper addresses the practical problem of converting RECIST measurements into 3D lesion segmentation while maintaining computational efficiency. The combination of RECIST-centered cropping, prompt-channel conditioning, and a lightweight MedNeXt-v2 backbone provides a clear approach to this task. The method is well explained, and Figures 1 and 2 help readers understand the overall framework and network structure. They clearly describe the core design of three channels of image, binary RECIST mask, and end point heatmap as well as the auxiliary head used.

The experimental evaluation is well organized. The authors compare five segmentation backbones and investigate the effects of auxiliary anatomy supervision and AutoZoom. In the architecture ablation (Table 4), MedNeXt-v2 f32 achieves the highest validation score of 71.40%, compared with 65.08% for SwinUNETR f24, while using only 6.92M parameters. The auxiliary supervision experiment is also useful because the authors acknowledge that the observed improvement is not conclusive. Another strength is the comparison with Lite ENSAM and the evaluation of computational efficiency. On the hidden test set, LETT-NeXt achieves a challenge score of 70.60%, compared with 62.15% for Lite ENSAM. The model also achieves a mean CPU inference time of 6.88 seconds per case on the public validation set, with a maximum resident memory of 3.57 GB. The qualitative examples in Figures 3 and 4 further illustrate successful predictions and failure cases.

Weaknesses: The benefit of AutoZoom is limited on the public validation set. In Table 6, the overall challenge score slightly decreases from 75.99% to 75.87% when AutoZoom is used. Although AutoZoom is designed to handle lesions extending beyond the initial crop, a more detailed comparison of the cases where AutoZoom is triggered would help explain when it improves or reduces segmentation quality.
